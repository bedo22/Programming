# 2. High-priority completeness gaps

These are the major strategic dimensions missing from the current draft.

## 2.1 Add: Competitive Strategy & Moats
The doc discusses market opportunity (TAM/SAM/SOM) but ignores **competition**. Why will this product win and stay winning? 
Add a section: **Competitive Advantage & Moats**

| Moat Type | What it is | Software Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Network Effects** | Value increases as more users join | Slack, Figma, Marketplaces |
| **Switching Costs** | Pain of leaving (data, workflow, integrations) | Salesforce, AWS, ERP systems |
| **Economies of Scale** | Unit costs drop as volume grows | Cloud infrastructure, payment processing |
| **Proprietary Data/Tech** | Unique assets competitors can't easily copy | Google Search graph, specialized AI models |
| **Brand / Community** | Trust and identity that lowers CAC | Apple, Stripe, developer communities |

*Key insight:* A product without a moat is just a feature waiting to be copied by an incumbent. Strategy must identify which moat the investment is building.

## 2.2 Add: Pricing, Packaging & Monetization
Section 3 covers unit economics (CAC/LTV), but it skips the mechanism of value capture. Pricing is the exchange rate of value; it dictates the entire product shape.
Add a subsection in §3: **Value Capture & Pricing Strategy**

| Model | How it works | Strategic implication |
|---|---|---|
| **Subscription (SaaS)** | Flat recurring fee (per seat or tier) | Predictable revenue; optimize for retention and seat expansion |
| **Usage-Based** | Pay for what you consume (API calls, compute) | Aligns cost with value; lower barrier to entry; revenue is volatile |
| **Freemium** | Free tier to acquire, paid tier to monetize | Product is the marketing; requires massive top-of-funnel volume |
| **Transactional** | Take rate on GMV or per-transaction fee | Requires high volume; aligns platform with user success |

## 2.3 Add: Portfolio Strategy & Product Lifecycle
The doc focuses heavily on evaluating *new* opportunities (0-to-1 or feature additions). It lacks a framework for managing a *portfolio* of existing products at different stages of maturity.
Add a section: **Portfolio Management & The Three Horizons**

| Horizon | Focus | Metric of Success | Investment Profile |
|---|---|---|---|
| **H1: Core** | Optimize and defend existing cash cows | Retention, Margin, Efficiency | High confidence, incremental innovation |
| **H2: Emerging** | Scale proven new products into mainstream | Growth rate, Market share | Medium confidence, scaling execution |
| **H3: Transformative** | Explore entirely new markets/models | Validated learning, Option value | Low confidence, high failure rate, R&D |

*Key insight:* Strategy isn't just picking the next feature; it's balancing the portfolio so the company survives today (H1) while inventing tomorrow (H3).

## 2.4 Add: Go-To-Market (GTM) & Distribution
A great product with no distribution strategy fails. The doc mentions Product-Led Growth (PLG) in the history, but needs a formal GTM section.
Add a section: **Distribution & Go-To-Market**

| GTM Motion | Who it's for | Strategic Constraint |
|---|---|---|
| **Product-Led (PLG)** | Low ACV, high volume, intuitive UX | Product must sell itself; requires massive top-of-funnel |
| **Sales-Led (Enterprise)** | High ACV, complex compliance/integration | CAC is high; requires sales engineering and long cycles |
| **Channel / Partner** | Reaching fragmented markets via resellers | You share margin; you lose direct user relationship |
| **Community / Content** | Developer tools, open-source | Slow compounding; requires authentic engagement |

# 3. Medium-priority improvements

## 3.1 Add: The "Kill" Decision (Sunsetting)
Strategy is what you *won't* do, but killing an *existing* product or feature is one of the hardest strategic acts. 
Add a subsection under §6 (Artifacts) or §9b (Failure modes):
> **Sunsetting and Deprecation:** Every product has a lifecycle. The strategic decision to kill a feature or sunset a product requires a "Deprecation Record" (analogous to an ADR). It must answer: Why is the maintenance cost now higher than the value? What is the migration path for users? What is the PR/communication plan? *Failure mode: The Zombie Product — kept alive because no one wants to make the hard call, draining engineering capacity from H1/H2 priorities.*

## 3.2 Add: Assumption Mapping & Risk (Pre-mortems)
Section 1e covers Expected Value and Cost of Delay, but doesn't cover how to map strategic risk before investing.
Add a brief mental model:
> **Assumption Mapping (Desirability/Feasibility/Viability vs. Known/Unknown):** Before building, map your assumptions. The highest-risk assumptions are those that are *Unknown* (we don't know if it's true) and *High Impact* (if it's false, the product fails). Strategy dictates that you test High-Impact/Unknown assumptions *first* (via prototypes, painted doors, or concierge tests) before writing production code.

## 3.3 Add: Standard Analytics Frameworks
In §9 (Measurement), mention standard frameworks to organize metrics so teams don't just track random numbers.
*   **AARRR (Pirate Metrics):** Acquisition, Activation, Retention, Referral, Revenue. (Good for PLG/Growth).
*   **HEART (Google):** Happiness, Engagement, Adoption, Retention, Task Success. (Good for UX-heavy internal or enterprise tools).
