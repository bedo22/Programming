## Context
The practical half of the process thread. L18 established what the SDLC and methodology are; this lesson teaches how Agile actually runs at work — Scrum's 3 roles / 5 events / 3 artifacts, Kanban's flow-vs-timebox contrast (WIP limits, pull not push), the failure modes (cargo cult Agile, velocity gaming, technical debt, burnout), and the AI frontier the learner specifically asked about. The learner originally asked about "AI development lifecycle"; we found the correct term is the **Agent Development Lifecycle (ADLC)**.

## Key insight
There are **two fundamentally different things** hiding inside "AI is changing software development". (1) **AI-augmented SDLC** — AI helps a human build traditional software faster (Copilot, code gen); the phases are the same, only the evaluation skill becomes premium (the AI produces more, so judging quality matters more — ties to L17 Solution Evaluation). (2) The **Agent Development Lifecycle (ADLC)** — building AI agents as a *new kind of software*: new phases (prompt/data/context curation), new guardrails (safety, tool access), new monitoring (runtime behaviour, drift), new evaluation (evals, not just tests). One accelerates the old; the other is genuinely new. Knowing which one people mean is the key to navigating the next 5 years.

## Why this matters for future sessions
Scrum's retrospective is the *metacognitive loop* at team scale (the same Schoenfeld metacognition from L13, applied to process). Kanban's WIP limits are a cognitive-load management principle (L15/L1e) applied to workflow — same theory, new scale. The ADLC is a genuinely new frontier in our map and is the natural entry point to the Economic reality movement (L20) — the income/markets around the ecosystem now includes AI and agent building. The Software Development Process reference is now a full 19-section standard-template doc, completing the macro-process layer of the map.
