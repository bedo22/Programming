# 0013 — Computational thinking, heuristics, and metacognition nest within Pólya

**Date:** 2026-08-04
**Lesson:** `lessons/0013-problem-solving-computational-thinking.html`
**Reference:** `reference/problem-solving.html` §3–§5

## Context
Lesson 12 gave Pólya's four phases (the "when"). This lesson fills the "what" (computational thinking's four pillars), the "how" (seven heuristics), and the "monitor" (Schoenfeld's metacognition).

## Key insight
The four frameworks **nest**, they don't compete:
- Pólya gives the **when** (the four-phase rhythm)
- Computational thinking gives the **what** (the four moves within the phases: decomposition, pattern recognition, abstraction, algorithm design)
- Heuristics give the **how** (the seven strategies: analogy, specialization, working backwards, decomposition, variation, auxiliary elements, generalization)
- Metacognition gives the **monitor** (checking if it's working)

Schoenfeld's empirical finding (1985): Pólya's heuristics alone are weak. Students who learned the strategies still failed on non-routine problems. The strategies need domain-specific tactics AND metacognition — monitoring your own thinking and adjusting as you work.

## Why this matters for future sessions
The metacognition concept connects directly to the rhythm's "feel the friction" signal (L15). Both are the same cognitive skill applied to different domains: algorithmic challenges (L13) and feature building (L15). Pattern recognition — the bridge between understanding and planning — is built through deliberate practice and pattern filing (L14).
